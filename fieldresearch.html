FIELD RESEARCH — INTERVIEW RECORD

Scrap Dealer Interview

Location: Pennathur district, Tamil Nadu Subject: scrap trading business

INTERVIEW WITH SCRAP DEALER

Recording of the full interview. Transcript below follows the same order.

REAL SOLUTION

Follow-up recording.

TRANSCRIPT

How long have you been doing the scrap business?

My father has been doing this business for 25 years, and I have been doing it for 6 years. So, totally, we have 31 years of experience.

How did you get into this business?

My father used to have a bicycle shop and an iron shop. He used to buy iron and sell it to other people. Later, he thought of starting his own iron business. Instead of buying from others, he started buying iron directly from the mills and selling it. Then we got a reference to a mill in Pondicherry. The mill asked us to supply scrap loads and told us that we could become dealers. My father started supplying scrap loads to them. Before GST was introduced, we had to handle the billing and payments for different sales offices. We used to visit different scrap shops. People from nearby villages such as Pennathur, Tirunelveli, and Keezhpennathur used to come to us. Some people collect scrap using bicycles as their main occupation. They would bring the scrap to us, and we would buy it from them.

So, they bring the scrap to you, and you buy it from them?

Yes. If someone has a large amount of scrap at home, we go and collect it. For example, if today's market price is ₹30, we may buy it for ₹25 or ₹26 and then sell it to recyclers for around ₹30 or ₹31. For motors, refrigerators, and ACs, copper can be around ₹1,300–₹1,400 per kg. There are different types of copper coils. Heavy copper coils have more thickness and are more valuable. They can be easily melted and reshaped. We also get glass bottles. If they are melted and sold, they can have higher value. However, in our area, we don't have approval to melt glass bottles. There is a company in Karnataka that melts glass bottles. We can clean the bottles using acid washing and reuse them for things like wine shops and other purposes.

In which type of scrap do you get the most profit?

We get more profit from heavy iron that can be melted.

How do you find out today's price for copper, aluminium, iron, PCB, and batteries?

In Tiruvannamalai, there is a dealer who specializes in copper. I ask them about the current copper price. For example, today's copper price is around ₹1,400 per kg.

Is the scrap quality usually correct, or is it mixed? Does that cause any problems?

We cannot do the testing ourselves. The people who buy the scrap from us will do the testing. When we take scrap from small shops, we sell it to recyclers. They separate the scrap into different categories, such as super melting and heavy melting materials. They check the quality and do the testing at the mill. We don't do the testing ourselves.

Do you get wires?

Yes, we get copper wires.

Do you burn them?

We can peel the wires, and peeled copper gets a higher price — around ₹100 more. If we burn the wires, the price is lower. But we receive copper wires in large quantities, so peeling all of them is difficult for us. That's why we sometimes burn them. We have a setup like a box where we burn the wires. There are also wire-peeling machines, but they are mainly suitable for thick wires, such as the main electrical wires used in houses. They cannot easily peel normal-sized wires. The quality and price are lower when the wires are processed this way. If we peel the wires properly, we can get a higher price.

If we create an app that shows the daily price of copper, aluminium, and iron, would it be useful for you?

Yes, it would be useful for all businesses. Big businesses won't be able to fool small businesses about the price. Even if they are not highly educated, they are trained to use phones. So, they can check the price directly in the app.
